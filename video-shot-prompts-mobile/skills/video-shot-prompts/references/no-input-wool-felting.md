## What to learn

Treat the reference as construction grammar, not a mandatory design to copy:

1. observe the live subject or a clear photo reference;
2. draw a proportion/template guide;
3. compact loose white wool into a flat head core;
4. needle-felt eye cavities, cheeks, and muzzle volumes;
5. install/refine eyes, nose, ears, and face planes;
6. wrap and compact wool around the body core;
7. attach and refine limbs, surface texture, and tail;
8. reveal the completed sculpture in the same ordinary craft workspace.

Retain the vertical smartphone character, real hands, needles and multi-needle tools, foam/work mat, loose fibers, visible partial completion, ordinary indoor light, and handmade irregularity. Do not turn the process into smooth plastic, synthetic plush, CGI, or polished studio photography unless requested.

## Storyboard construction

- Create 6-8 shots; default to 8 when the user asks for the full making process.
- If the user names a subject, adapt anatomy, markings, and proportions to it while preserving physically plausible needle-felting stages.
- If no subject is named, use a white Devon-style felt cat as a study and say that it is the default study subject.
- Build the final identity anchor first. Use it as identity reference for earlier previews while explicitly replacing the finished state with the correct unfinished stage.
- Every shot must state: existing state, active hand/tool action, material transition, end state, framing, and the feature that must carry into the next shot.
- Output a Chinese shot title and explanation, an English generation prompt, and a concise English negative prompt.
- Do not include fake source timestamps. Label the sequence `概念分镜`.
- Prefer native ImageGen for the identity anchor and every preview. Use APIYi only when ImageGen is unavailable; do not automatically retry billable requests.
